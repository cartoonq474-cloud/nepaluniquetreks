Founder's Story & Philosophy

Walking Beside You on Every Step — From Mountain Trails to Founder

Raised in the rugged foothills of the Annapurna range, Dil Gurung didn't learn the Himalayas from maps or guidebooks — he lived them. Beginning his career as a young trail porter, he carried expedition gear over 5,000-meter frozen passes, learning firsthand the physical demands, the vulnerability, and the sacred spirit of the high mountains.

Over 15 years, Dil advanced from porter to licensed mountain guide and senior expedition leader, guiding hundreds of international adventurers across Everest, Annapurna, Manaslu, and unexplored western valleys. Through this journey, he saw an industry that too often prioritized commercial volume over client acclimatization safety and fair porter treatment.

In 2014, Dil founded Nepal Unique Treks with a simple, transformative ethos: provide direct, honest, transparent Himalayan adventures with zero middlemen, direct founder involvement in every itinerary, and uncompromising respect for the people who call the mountains home.

“The Himalayas do not belong to any travel agency; they belong to nature and the resilient communities living among these sacred peaks. When you travel with us, you are not just a customer — you are welcomed as family. Every itinerary is crafted for your safety, and every mountain porter is safeguarded with warmth, fair pay, and dignity.”

Our Safety Standard

The 6-Step High-Altitude Safety Chain

Every trek we operate follows an uncompromising 6-stage safety protocol, ensuring you summit and cross high passes with peace of mind.

Step 01 • Planning

Tailor-Made Acclimatization Pacing

Routes engineered around UIAA medical guidelines: conservative elevation gain caps (max 300–500m sleeping altitude gain per day above 3,000m) with dedicated active rest days.

Step 02 • Briefing

Kathmandu Gear Check & Orientation

Comprehensive face-to-face briefing at our Thamel office before departure. Our lead guides check your 4-layer clothing system, sleeping bag rating, and pharmacy supplies.

Step 03 • Leadership

Certified Wilderness First Responders

Every group is led by a government-licensed senior Sherpa guide certified in Wilderness First Aid (WFA/WFR), high-altitude pharmacology, and crevasse rescue protocols.

Step 04 • Daily Care

Twice-Daily Pulse Oximeter Vitals

Morning and evening biometric saturation checks tracking SpO2 oxygen levels, resting heart rates, and clinical Lake Louise Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) scores.

Step 05 • Crew Care

Strict Porter Welfare & Weight Limits

Maximum 20kg duffel weight limit per porter, warm alpine footwear and gear, dignified teahouse lodging, and comprehensive emergency medical and evacuation coverage.

Step 06 • Rescue

24/7 Kathmandu Dispatch & Heli Evac

Direct radio and Garmin inReach satellite link with our Kathmandu operations desk. Immediate coordination with Kathmandu helicopter rescue operators and CIWEC Hospital.

Trail Routine

A Day in the Life on a Nepal Unique Trek

Wondering what daily rhythm awaits you among 8,000-meter giants? Here is how a classic day on the trail unfolds with our guides.

06:30 Morning

Mountain Sunrise & Bedside Tea

Wake up to a gentle knock on your teahouse door and a piping hot cup of black, ginger, or milk tea. Step onto your balcony to witness golden alpenglow illuminating giant snow peaks.

07:15 Morning

Health Vitals & Hearty Teahouse Breakfast

Your lead guide checks your SpO2 blood oxygen saturation and resting pulse with a digital oximeter. Enjoy a warm breakfast of porridge, farm-fresh eggs, Tibetan flatbread, and freshly brewed coffee.

08:15 Morning

Scenic Mountain March at Sherpa Pace

Hit the trail under clear blue morning skies. We hike at a steady, rhythmic cadence ("Bistari, Bistari" — slowly, slowly in Nepali), pausing for photo stops, suspension bridges, and wildlife sightings.

12:30 Midday

Warm Hamlet Lunch & Trail Rest

Stop at a sunny hamlet teahouse terrace for a freshly prepared hot lunch. Refuel with hearty Dal Bhat (steamed rice, yellow lentil soup, curried vegetables), noodle soup, or vegetable fried rice.

15:30 Afternoon

Teahouse Arrival & Warm Unwinding

Arrive at your afternoon destination lodge. Settle into your twin room, change into cozy dry fleece, and join fellow trekkers in the wood- or yak-dung-heated central dining hall with hot lemon honey tea.

18:30 Evening

Communal Dinner & Tomorrow's Route Briefing

Enjoy a hearty dinner surrounded by global travelers. Dil or your lead guide reviews tomorrow's route elevation profile, terrain hazards, weather forecasts, and performs your evening oxygen check.

20:30 Night

Deep Rest Under Starlit Himalayan Skies

Tuck into your cozy sub-zero sleeping bag. Far from city lights, the mountain silence and brilliant Milky Way canopy create the deepest, most restorative sleep of your life.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers about Nepal Unique Treks, our safety policies, booking with Dil Gurung, and what sets our team apart.

How does booking directly with Nepal Unique Treks compare to international agencies? ▼

When you book with Nepal Unique Treks, you are communicating directly with Founder Dil Gurung and our local operations team in Kathmandu with zero middlemen commissions. Overseas travel agencies often outsource operations to third-party local operators like us while adding a 30% to 50% markup. Booking directly with us ensures 100% of your payment supports local guides, porters, and high-quality teahouses, while giving you complete flexibility to tailor your dates, rest days, and pacing.

What specific training do your trekking guides possess? ▼

All Nepal Unique Treks lead guides are officially licensed by the Government of Nepal Ministry of Tourism and certified by the Nepal Academy of Tourism and Hotel Management (NATHM). Furthermore, they undergo regular certifications in Wilderness First Aid (WFR/WFA), high-altitude acute mountain sickness (AMS) diagnosis, pulse oximetry tracking, and environmental Leave No Trace protocols. Our peak climbing leaders also hold specialized technical climbing credentials from the Nepal Mountaineering Association (NMA).

How do you handle acute altitude sickness (AMS) and emergency evacuations? ▼

Prevention is always our primary line of defense: we design generous acclimatization schedules, monitor twice-daily biometric pulse oximeter saturation, and pace ascents deliberately. If a trekker develops moderate to severe AMS symptoms, the rule is immediate descent. Our guides carry satellite communication devices (Garmin inReach) connected directly to our Kathmandu command desk, allowing us to initiate priority emergency helicopter evacuations with pre-cleared charter operators within minutes, directly admitting patients to Kathmandu's specialized CIWEC Hospital.

What is your policy regarding porter welfare and fair working conditions? ▼

Because our founder Dil Gurung began his career as an expedition porter, our company has one of the strictest porter welfare charters in Nepal. We enforce a strict 20kg maximum load cap per porter, pay wages significantly higher than statutory minimums, provide warm technical mountain gear (down jackets, alpine boots, thermal layers, UV sunglasses), provide comprehensive medical insurance, and ensure that our porters receive the exact same quality of food and warm lodge bedding as our guests.

Can I customize my trek dates, itinerary pacing, or request a private trip? ▼

Absolutely. Over 70% of our expeditions are private tailor-made journeys. Whether you are traveling solo, as a couple on a honeymoon, with family, or in a private club, Dil Gurung will collaborate directly with you to craft an itinerary suited to your specific fitness, available calendar days, and comfort preferences — including luxury lodge upgrades, helicopter return flights, or side acclimatization hikes.

Where is your Kathmandu office located and can we meet before our trek? ▼

Our head office is conveniently located at 16 - Sorhakhutte, Thamel, Kathmandu, right in the heart of the main trekking district. We encourage all travelers to visit our office upon arrival in Kathmandu for a warm cup of Himalayan tea, in-person gear inspection, route briefing, and a chance to meet your lead guide and Dil Gurung before heading to the trailhead.
