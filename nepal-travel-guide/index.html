Climate Matrix

When to Trek in Nepal: Seasonal Breakdown

Nepal's Himalayan climate is governed by elevation and the Indian Ocean monsoon. Selecting the right trekking window ensures crystal-clear panoramic visibility, safe high-pass crossings, and comfortable teahouse nights.

Autumn: The Gold Standard for Himalayan Trekking

Post-monsoon high-pressure systems bring the clearest skies and crispest views of the entire year.

🍂🏔️
🌡️ Temperatures
Day: 12°C to 18°C (3,000m)
Night: -5°C to -10°C (5,000m)
👁️ Visibility & Weather
95% Crystal Clear Days
Minimal rainfall, stable high passes
Recommended Routes:
Everest Base Camp (14D) Manaslu Circuit (13D) Annapurna Circuit (13D) Gokyo Lakes (12D)
💡

Senior Sherpa Pro Tip

Because October is the peak trekking month in Nepal, tea houses along the classic Everest and Annapurna trails fill up weeks in advance. Book by July or August to secure prime heated rooms with mountain views.

Spring: Wildflowers & Warmer High Passes

Hillsides burst with blooming red and pink rhododendron forests, with longer sunny daytime hiking hours.

🌸🏔️
🌡️ Temperatures
Day: 15°C to 22°C (3,000m)
Night: -2°C to -6°C (5,000m)
👁️ Visibility & Weather
Clear Mornings, Afternoon Clouds
Warming high passes (Thorong La & Cho La)
Recommended Routes:
Annapurna Base Camp (12D) Langtang Valley (8D) Mardi Himal Ridge (6D) Mera Peak Climbing (18D)
💡

Senior Sherpa Pro Tip

Spring mornings offer pristine 360-degree clarity. Plan to start hiking by 7:30 AM to reach your destination tea house by early afternoon before typical thermal clouds drift in.

Monsoon: Trans-Himalayan Rainshadow Treks

While southern valleys get rain, regions north of the main Himalayan chain remain dry, sunny, and culturally sublime.

☀️🏜️
🌡️ Temperatures
Day: 18°C to 26°C (Mustang)
Night: 6°C to 10°C (Mustang)
👁️ Rainshadow Protection
Virtually Zero Rain in Mustang
Blocked by Annapurna & Dhaulagiri barriers
Recommended Rainshadow Treks:
Upper Mustang Kingdom (14D) Upper Dolpo Circuit (24D) Nar Phu Valley (16D)
💡

Senior Sherpa Pro Tip

Avoid classic Everest or Langtang trails in July/August due to trail leeches and cloud cover. Instead, choose Upper Mustang for dry desert canyon scenery, Tibetan Buddhist monasteries, and summer horse festivals!

Winter: Crisp Skies & Empty Trails

Unrivaled deep blue skies and peaceful lodges with zero crowds. Ideal for treks below 4,000m.

❄️☀️
🌡️ Temperatures
Day: 8°C to 14°C (2,500m)
Night: -15°C to -20°C (High lodges)
👁️ Trail Traffic
Low Crowds (Empty Trails)
Deep snow on 5,000m high passes
Recommended Winter Treks:
Ghorepani Poon Hill (4D) Everest Panorama (7D) Pikey Peak Panorama (9D) Tamang Heritage Trail (8D)
💡

Senior Sherpa Pro Tip

If trekking in winter, pack a heavy -15°C to -20°C goose down sleeping bag and thermal hot water bottle. Teahouses do not have bedroom heating, only wood-burning stoves in the communal dining hall!

Mountain Medicine

High-Altitude Safety: The AMS Acclimatization Center

Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) is caused by reduced atmospheric air pressure and lower oxygen saturation at elevations above 2,500m (8,200ft). It strikes independently of fitness, athletic endurance, or age. Acclimatization is a biological adaptation that requires patience and strict protocols.

0m – 2,500m
Safe Elevation Zone
Kathmandu (1,400m) & Pokhara (820m). Normal physiological oxygen saturation (98-100%). Minimal AMS risk.
2,500m – 3,500m
High Altitude Zone
Namche Bazaar (3,440m). AMS onset threshold begins. Ascent must be capped at 400m-500m sleeping elevation per day.
3,500m – 5,500m
Very High Altitude
Dingboche, Thorong Phedi, EBC (5,364m). Hypoxemia risk. Mandatory rest days every 1,000m gained. Daily SpO2 monitoring.
5,500m+
Extreme Altitude
Kala Patthar (5,550m), Thorong La (5,416m). Oxygen is ~50% of sea level. Prolonged stays hazardous; ascend, view, descend immediately.
⚠️

The Non-Negotiable Mountain Law

Never ascend with symptoms of altitude sickness. If a trekker experiences a persistent headache, nausea, or dizziness, they must rest at that altitude. If symptoms do not improve within 24 hours, or if they worsen, immediate descent of at least 500m to 1,000m is mandatory.

5 Golden Rules of Himalayan Acclimatization

💧
Hydrate Aggressively (3–4L)

Dry mountain air dehydrates your lungs rapidly. Drink 3 to 4 liters of clean water, electrolyte mixes, and warm ginger or garlic soup daily.

🚶‍♂️
"Bistari, Bistari" (Go Slowly)

In Nepali, "Bistari" means slowly. Maintain an easy, steady conversational pace where your breathing remains effortless throughout uphill climbs.

🛌
Climb High, Sleep Low

Hike 300m above your night lodge in the late afternoon, take in the views for 45 minutes, then descend to sleep. This triggers red blood cell production.

🩺
Daily Pulse Oximeter Checks

Our certified guides record your resting heart rate and blood oxygen saturation (SpO2) every morning and evening to catch early signs of AMS.

Mild AMS Symptoms vs. Emergency HAPE / HACE Triggers

🟡 Mild to Moderate AMS
  • Dull throbbing headache behind the eyes
  • Loss of appetite, mild nausea
  • Insomnia or restless sleep
  • Lightheadedness upon standing
  • Action: Rest, hydrate, consider Diamox 125mg, do not ascend.
🔴 Emergency HAPE / HACE (Life Threatening)
  • Wet, gurgling breath sound or pink frothy sputum (HAPE)
  • Severe breathlessness while completely resting
  • Ataxia: Loss of balance or inability to walk heel-to-toe
  • Extreme confusion, slurred speech, delirium (HACE)
  • Action: Immediate descent, supplemental O2, emergency helicopter dispatch.

🏧

Kathmandu ATM Cash Reality Check

Kathmandu and Pokhara have hundreds of reliable ATMs (Nabil Bank, Standard Chartered, Himalayan Bank). However, past Namche Bazaar or Besisahar, ATMs are non-existent or frequently broken due to power and satellite failures. Always withdraw NPR 25,000 to 35,000 (~$190 to $270 USD) in cash per trekker in Kathmandu before heading into the mountains.

Respect & Traditions

Cultural Etiquette & Trail Ethics: The Mountain Dos & Don'ts

The Himalayas are not merely scenic mountain ridges; they are living spiritual sanctuaries inhabited by Sherpa, Tamang, Gurung, Rai, and Thakali communities. Embracing sacred customs ensures meaningful, respectful connections with the mountain people.

✅ Trail & Cultural DOs
  • ✓
    Walk Clockwise Around Stupas: Always pass Buddhist chortens, stupas, and carved mani stone walls on the left so the sacred monument remains on your right. Spin prayer wheels clockwise.
  • ✓
    Stand on the Mountain Side for Pack Animals: When yak or mule caravans approach, always step to the uphill/mountain slope side. Stepping to the cliff/valley edge risks being knocked down steep precipices.
  • ✓
    Use the "Namaste" Greeting: Press both palms together at your chest and bow slightly. It is the universal sign of respect across all Nepali cultures.
  • ✓
    Remove Shoes in Gompas: Take off trekking boots and hats before stepping inside Buddhist monasteries, Hindu shrines, or private village family living quarters.
  • ✓
    Pack Out All Plastic Trash: Leave No Trace. Carry candy wrappers, blister pill foils, and battery waste back down to designated collection centers in Lukla or Kathmandu.
❌ Mountain & Trail DON'Ts
  • ✗
    Never Step Over Sacred Items: Avoid stepping over monks' cushions, prayer books, hearths, or religious relics. Pointing the soles of your feet toward people or altars is considered offensive.
  • ✗
    Don't Photograph Without Asking: Always ask courteous permission before photographing monks, women washing clothes, or local children. Respect photography bans inside ancient monastery shrines.
  • ✗
    Don't Hand Out Candy or Cash to Kids: Giving candy creates tooth decay without dental access; giving money encourages begging. Instead, donate books and notebooks directly to headmasters at local trail schools.
  • ✗
    Never Swim in Sacred Lakes: High-altitude glacial waters like Gokyo Sacred Lakes, Gosaikunda, and Tilicho Lake are revered holy sites. Swimming or washing laundry in them is strictly forbidden.
  • ✗
    Don't Use Your Left Hand for Food: When passing food, money, or shaking hands, use your right hand (or both hands for polite deference). The left hand is traditionally reserved for sanitary purposes.
Flight Operations

Domestic Mountain Flights & The Kathmandu Buffer Day Rule

High-altitude mountain runways such as Lukla (Tenzing-Hillary Airport for Everest) and Jomsom (Mustang) sit in narrow glacial valleys without radar or automated instrument landing systems. Flights operate solely under strict Visual Flight Rules (VFR). Understanding flight logistics prevents travel stress.

01

Strict VFR Weather Rules

If dense fog, low cloud cover, or strong crosswinds occur at Lukla, flights are immediately held in Kathmandu or Ramechhap for safety until conditions clear.

02

Seasonal Ramechhap Routing

During peak Spring and Autumn seasons, all Lukla passenger flights shift from KTM to Ramechhap (Manthali Airport) to avoid Kathmandu international runway congestion. A 4-hour pre-dawn shuttle is arranged.

03

Helicopter Sharing Backup

When airplanes cannot fly due to low cloud ceiling, helicopters can often fly through lower river valleys under marginal visual conditions. Seat-sharing costs approximately $350–$500 per passenger.

04

Mandatory Buffer Days

Always schedule at least 1 to 2 buffer days in Kathmandu after your trek before your international flight home. This protects your departure tickets if mountain flights encounter weather delays.
