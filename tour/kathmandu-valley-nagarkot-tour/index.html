Kathmandu Valley & Nagarkot Tour Overview

Discover the best of Nepal with this classic Kathmandu Valley Day Tour extended to the hill station of Nagarkot a perfect blend of culture, history, and Himalayan scenery. Ideal as a short Tour in Nepal or as an add-on to a longer Trekking in Nepal, this UNESCO World Heritage Tour takes you through centuries-old temples, palace squares, and traditional Newari towns before winding uphill for one of the valley's finest mountain panoramas.

Kathmandu Valley

Kathmandu Valley is the cultural and historical heart of Nepal, home to the capital city Kathmandu along with the twin cities of Patan (Lalitpur) and Bhaktapur. Nestled in the mid-hills at roughly 1,400 meters, the valley has been a crossroads of trade, religion, and art for over a thousand years, blending Hindu and Buddhist traditions into a unique living heritage.

It holds seven UNESCO World Heritage Sites within a compact area: the three Durbar Squares (Kathmandu, Patan, Bhaktapur), the stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath, and the temples of Pashupatinath and Changu Narayan. Narrow alleys, wood-carved windows, pagoda-style temples, and bustling local markets give the valley a distinct medieval charm that coexists with modern city life.

Ringed by green hills including popular viewpoints like Nagarkot and Dhulikhel Kathmandu Valley also serves as the main gateway for travelers heading onward to trek in the Himalayas, fly to Pokhara or Lumbini, or explore rural Nepal, making it the natural starting and ending point for most Nepal itineraries.

Nagarkot

Nagarkot is a popular hill station located about 32 km east of Kathmandu, perched at an elevation of around 2,175 meters on the rim of Kathmandu Valley. It's renowned as one of the best viewpoints in the region for spectacular Himalayan panoramas, offering sightings of Mt. Everest, Langtang, Ganesh Himal, and other peaks on clear days.

Once a strategic retreat for Nepali royalty, Nagarkot today draws travelers for its peaceful atmosphere, cool climate, and stunning sunrise and sunset views over layers of misty hills and distant snow-capped mountains. It's a favorite short escape from the busy streets of Kathmandu, ideal for a quiet overnight stay, light hiking, birdwatching, or simply relaxing at a hillside lodge with mountain-facing rooms.

Nagarkot is commonly paired with a Kathmandu Valley Day Tour or included as part of a longer Nepal itinerary, making it a natural extension for travelers seeking both cultural heritage in the valley below and a taste of the Himalayas without a full trek.

Namo Buddha

Namo Buddha is a sacred Buddhist pilgrimage site set on a forested ridge about 40 km from Kathmandu, at roughly 1,750 meters elevation. According to legend, it marks the spot where Prince Mahasattva offered his own body to save a starving tigress and her cubs an act of compassion central to Jataka tales of the Buddha's past lives.

The site is home to a prominent stupa and the Thrangu Tashi Yangtse Monastery, a large working monastery where visitors can observe monks in prayer and enjoy sweeping valley views. It's a meaningful stop for those interested in a Buddhist Pilgrimage Tour or a quiet Meditation Retreat Tour in Nepal.

Dhulikhel

Dhulikhel is a traditional Newari hill town about 30 km from Kathmandu, sitting at around 1,550 meters. Known for its well-preserved old town with intricately carved windows, temples, and courtyards, it also offers fine Himalayan views, including the Langtang range on clear days. Dhulikhel is popular for short hikes, cultural walks through its heritage core, and as a relaxed base for exploring the surrounding countryside making it a nice fit for a Village Tour or a slower-paced Nepal Experience Tour.

Together, Namo Buddha and Dhulikhel make an excellent 1–2 day extension to a Kathmandu Valley itinerary, combining spirituality, Newari heritage, and mountain scenery in a quieter, less-crowded setting than Nagarkot.

Best Time to Visit Kathmandu Valley Nagarkot Tour

Autumn (late September to November)

The best overall season. Skies are clear after the monsoon, temperatures are mild (10–25°C), and mountain views are at their sharpest. This period also overlaps with major festivals like Dashain and Tihar, great for a Festival Holiday in Nepal.

Spring (March to May)

The second-best window. Warm days, blooming rhododendrons in the hills, and generally good visibility for sunrise/sunset views from Nagarkot, though some haze can build up by late spring.

Winter (December to February)

Cold, especially in the mornings and evenings at higher spots like Nagarkot, but skies are often crisp and clear with excellent mountain visibility. Good for travelers who don't mind the chill.

Monsoon (June to August)

The least favorable time. Heavy rain, cloud cover, and leech-prone trails often block mountain views entirely, though the valley turns lush and green. Best avoided unless you're flexible about visibility.

Food & Accommodation

Kathmandu

Kathmandu offers the widest range of both. Accommodation spans budget guesthouses in Thamel to boutique hotels and international five-star chains. Food options are equally diverse authentic Newari cuisine (yomari, bara, choila), traditional dal bhat, momos, and a full range of international restaurants, cafés, and bakeries catering to all tastes and budgets.

Nagarkot

Accommodation here is mostly hillside resorts and lodges, many with mountain-facing rooms and rooftop terraces designed for sunrise/sunset viewing. Options range from simple guesthouses to comfortable mid-range and upscale resorts. Dining is generally hotel-based, offering Nepali, Tibetan, and continental meals hearty dal bhat and thukpa are common choices to warm up in the cooler hill air.

Dhulikhel

A mix of heritage guesthouses in the old town and resort-style hotels on the outskirts with valley and mountain views. Local eateries serve traditional Newari and Nepali dishes, while resort restaurants typically offer broader continental and Nepali menus.

Namo Buddha

Accommodation is limited and simple a few guesthouses and monastery-run lodges near the stupa, plus one or two retreat-style properties for those on a meditation stay. Meals tend to be basic vegetarian Nepali or Tibetan fare, fitting for the site's spiritual, low-key atmosphere.

Kathmandu Valley Nagarkot Tour Itinerary: Day by Day

6 Days program for Kathmandu Valley Nagarkot Tour. Daily timings and sequences remain flexible according to weather, flight operations, and group pace.

Day 1: Arrival in Kathmandu, 1400m

Approx. 1,400 m / 4,593 ft

Accommodation: 3-star Hotel

Arrive at Tribhuvan International Airport, where you'll be met and transferred to your hotel in Kathmandu. Depending on your arrival time, spend the rest of the day settling in and acclimatizing. If time allows, take a short evening stroll around Thamel or Durbar Marg to get a first feel for the city its narrow lanes, prayer flags, incense shops, and street food stalls. Overnight in Kathmandu.

Day 2: Kathmandu Valley Sightseeing, 5/6 hrs, 1400 m

Approx. 1,400 m / 4,593 ft

Duration: 5/6 hrs
Accommodation: 3-star Categories Hotel
Meals: Breakfast

A full day exploring the cultural heart of the valley. Typical stops include: Pashupatinath Temple the most sacred Hindu temple in Nepal, on the banks of the Bagmati River, where you can observe cremation rituals and sadhus (holy men). Boudhanath Stupa one of the largest stupas in the world and a major center of Tibetan Buddhism; walk the kora (circumambulation path) among maroon-robed monks and spinning prayer wheels. Kathmandu Durbar Square the old royal palace complex, home to the Kumari Ghar (residence of the Living Goddess), intricately carved temples, and courtyards. Swayambhunath (Monkey Temple) perched on a hilltop with panoramic views over the Kathmandu Valley, famous for its all-seeing Buddha eyes and resident monkeys. Overnight in Kathmandu.

Day 3: Patan & Bhaktapur, then Drive to Nagarkot, 5/6 hrs, 2175m

Approx. 2,175 m / 7,136 ft

Duration: 5/6 hrs
Accommodation: 3-star Categories Hotel
Meals: Breakfast, Dinner

Morning visit to Patan Durbar Square, just across the Bagmati River from Kathmandu, known for its concentration of Newari craftsmanship, the Patan Museum, and metal and stone artistry that reflects centuries of skilled artisanship. Continue to Bhaktapur Durbar Square, the best-preserved of the valley's three medieval royal cities. Wander its pottery square, the 55-Window Palace, Nyatapola Temple, and streets lined with traditional Newari architecture, wood carving, and local craft shops. In the afternoon, drive roughly 1.5 hours out of the valley up to Nagarkot, a hill station perched on the eastern rim of the Kathmandu Valley, renowned for sunrise and sunset views over the Himalayan range, including Everest on a clear day. Overnight in Nagarkot.

Day 4: Nagarkot to Namobuddha Monastery, Overnight in Dhulikhel, 5/6 hrs, 1550 m

Approx. 1,550 m / 5,085 ft

Duration: 5/6 hrs
Accommodation: 3-star Categories Hotel
Meals: Breakfast

Early risers can catch the sunrise over the Himalayas from Nagarkot before breakfast. Afterward, drive or hike toward Namobuddha, an important Buddhist pilgrimage site associated with the legend of the Bodhisattva who offered his body to a starving tigress. Visit the Namobuddha Monastery, take in the serene hilltop setting, mountain views, and terraced countryside along the way this stretch is also popular for gentle valley walks passing through traditional villages. Continue to Dhulikhel, a charming Newari town known for its old bazaar, temples, and views of the Langtang and Everest ranges. Overnight in Dhulikhel.

Day 5: Dhulikhel to Kathmandu, 1400 m

Approx. 1,400 m / 4,593 ft

Accommodation: 3-star Hotel
Meals: Breakfast

Morning free to explore Dhulikhel's old town, its narrow lanes, temples, and viewpoints, before driving back to Kathmandu (roughly 2 hours). Depending on time, use the afternoon for last-minute souvenir shopping in Thamel, or relax at your hotel. Overnight in Kathmandu.

Day 6: Departure from Kathmandu 1400 m

Approx. 1,400 m / 4,593 ft

Accommodation: 3-star Hotel
Meals: Breakfast

Free time until your scheduled transfer to Tribhuvan International Airport for your onward flight, marking the end of the tour.
