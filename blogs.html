Frequently Asked Himalayan Questions

Common questions answered by our Sherpa guides before you pack your bags for Nepal.

Yes. Since April 2023, the Nepal Tourism Board (NTB) mandated licensed guides for international solo trekkers across most major national parks and conservation areas to enhance mountain safety, prevent lost hiker incidents, and protect local mountain communities.
October and November (Autumn) offer the highest statistical probability of cloudless skies, razor-sharp mountain horizons, and mild daytime hiking conditions. March and April (Spring) are also premier months with warmer temperatures and blooming wild rhododendron forests.
You do not need to be an ultra-marathoner, but solid cardiovascular endurance and leg stamina are essential. You should be comfortable hiking 5 to 7 hours uphill daily with a 5-6 kg daypack. Training should include stair climbing, weighted backpack walks, and cycling for 8-12 weeks prior to departure.
Our expedition guides carry medical pulse oximeters, portable oxygen bottles, and emergency medical kits. The fundamental golden rule is to never ascend with symptoms. If symptoms persist or worsen, our team initiates immediate supervised descent and can coordinate 24/7 helicopter evacuation if medically necessary.
We recommend carrying approximately NPR 3,000 to 4,500 ($25-$35 USD) per person per day to cover hot showers, device charging, boiled drinking water, snacks, Wi-Fi cards, and beverage extras, as ATMs do not exist at higher altitudes.
Yes, teahouses offer solar and micro-hydro charging, usually in the communal dining hall. Charges range from NPR 200 to NPR 500 per device or power bank. We strongly recommend packing a 20,000mAh power bank and keeping batteries inside your sleeping bag at night to prevent cold-induced drainage.
Tipping is a customary and deeply appreciated gesture for hardworking mountain crews. As a general guideline, groups typically budget 10% to 15% of the total trip cost as a collective tip, divided among the lead guide, assistant guides, and porters on the final evening of the trek.
Always bring well-broken-in hiking boots, socks, and technical base layers from home. Bulky items like 4-season down jackets, sub-zero sleeping bags, and trekking poles can be rented or purchased affordably in Kathmandu's Thamel trekking district.
