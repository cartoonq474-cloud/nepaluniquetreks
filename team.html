Our Non-Negotiable Standards

What Sets a Nepal Unique Treks Leader Apart

We do not compromise on guide qualifications or trail safety. Every team member undergoes continuous alpine training and holds accredited certifications.

Govt. Licensed & Verified

100% of our lead guides are licensed by the Nepal Academy of Tourism and Hotel Management (NATHM) and accredited by the Trekking Agencies' Association of Nepal (TAAN).

Daily SpO2 Medical Vitals

Every morning and evening, your guide measures blood oxygen levels (SpO2) and resting pulse using clinical digital oximeters to track acclimatization before advancing.

Satellite Comms & Rescue

Equipped with Garmin inReach 2-way satellite devices and 24/7 Kathmandu operations liaison for instantaneous helicopter rescue authorization in high-alpine passes.

Native Heritage & Dialects

Our guides are native to the high valleys they lead—Sherpa, Tamang, and Gurung leaders with deep local ties to teahouse hosts, lamas, and mountain communities.

Ethical Tourism Commitment

Our Porter Welfare Charter: Zero Exploitation

A Himalayan expedition is only as honorable as how it treats its hardest working members. As a company founded by a former porter, our commitment to field staff is sacred.

Respecting the Real Heroes of the Trail

In many budget agencies, porters carry overloaded packs up to 35kg without proper insurance or warm footwear. Dil Gurung experienced this early in his career and vowed that Nepal Unique Treks would maintain the strictest ethical welfare charter in Nepal.

Every porter on our team is treated as family, provided comprehensive high-altitude medical evacuation insurance, and paid wages significantly higher than industry averages.

Behind the Scenes

A Day on the Mountain With Your Lead Guide

What does dedicated, thoughtful mountain leadership look like in practice? Here is how your guide coordinates your daily safety and comfort across the high Himalayas.

06:00 AM

Morning Vitals & Tea

Your guide serves warm ginger lemon tea, measures your SpO2 oxygen levels with a pulse oximeter, and reviews your sleep and hydration quality.

07:30 AM

Trail Departure & Pacing

Steps set to an easy, sustainable rhythm (bistari, bistari). Your guide reads trail conditions, points out sacred peaks, and manages hydration pauses.

12:30 PM

Nutritious Hot Lunch

A pre-booked stop at verified hygienic teahouses. Freshly cooked organic Dal Bhat or noodle soup to replenish carbs and essential electrolytes.

03:30 PM

Acclimatization Walk

Following the golden rule: “Climb high, sleep low.” An optional light 45-minute stroll up to a nearby viewpoint or monastery before dinner.

07:00 PM

Evening Briefing

Second vitals check in the dining hall, weather forecast review for passes like Cho La or Thorong La, and clothing plan for tomorrow’s ascent.
