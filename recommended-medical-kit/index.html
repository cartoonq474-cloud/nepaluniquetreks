💊 1. High-Altitude Medications & Diamox

Critical

Gradual acclimatization is always the primary prevention for altitude sickness. However, pharmacological aids are indispensable when ascending above 2,800m–3,000m in the Everest, Annapurna, and Manaslu circuits.

💊

Acetazolamide (Diamox)

Generic: Acetazolamide • Cipla / Sun Pharma • 125mg & 250mg tablets
Essential OTC in Nepal Rx in US/EU
Prophylactic Dose 125mg twice daily
Treatment Dose 250mg twice daily
Start Timing 24 hrs before 2,800m
Route Oral with water

How It Works: Diamox is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor. It causes the kidneys to excrete bicarbonate, gently acidifying your blood. This metabolic acidosis signals your brain's respiratory center to breathe deeper and faster—especially during sleep when high-altitude Cheyne-Stokes breathing typically causes blood oxygen drops. It accelerates natural acclimatization by 24 to 48 hours.

Standard Prophylactic Schedule: Take 125mg (half of a 250mg tablet) in the morning and 125mg at early dinner (~5:00 PM). Continue until you reach your trek's highest sleeping elevation and begin your permanent descent.

Normal, Benign Side Effects: Mild tingling in fingers, toes, and lips (paresthesia), increased urine output (diuresis), and carbonated beverages tasting flat (beer or soda). These are harmless physiological responses.

⚠️ Critical Sulfa Drug Allergy Warning: Acetazolamide is a sulfonamide derivative. If you have a confirmed allergy to sulfa medications, you must NOT take Diamox without explicit prior clearance from your allergist or physician.
💡 Lead Sherpa Pro Tip: Take your evening dose at dinner (~5:00 PM) rather than right before crawling into your sleeping bag at 9:00 PM. Because Diamox is a mild diuretic, early evening administration helps prevent waking up at 2:00 AM to use freezing teahouse outhouses!
🚨

Dexamethasone (Decadron 4mg)

Potent Corticosteroid • High-Altitude Emergency Treatment
Strict Prescription Guide Emergency Pouch
Emergency Dose 4mg to 8mg stat, then 4mg q6h
Indication HACE / Severe AMS
Mechanism Reduces cerebral brain swelling
Action Requirement Immediate descent mandatory

Clinical Role: Dexamethasone does not acclimatize the body; it temporizes life-threatening cerebral edema (HACE) by reducing brain swelling and capillary permeability. It restores neurological clarity so an afflicted trekker can walk downhill under their own power during an emergency descent.

Who Carries It: Your Nepal Unique Treks certified mountain guide carries Dexamethasone in the master expedition trauma kit. Trekkers ascending past 5,000m passes (Thorong La, Cho La, Kongma La) may also request a prescription 3-day emergency pack from their travel doctor.

🚨 Never Ascend on Dexamethasone: Dexamethasone masks severe altitude symptoms. Taking it to continue climbing can cause catastrophic neurological collapse. Once administered, immediate downhill descent is compulsory.
🫁

Nifedipine (Extended Release 20mg–30mg)

Calcium Channel Blocker • Pulmonary Vasodilator for HAPE
Prescription HAPE Specific

Clinical Role: Nifedipine dilates constricted pulmonary blood vessels, rapidly lowering elevated pulmonary artery pressure to prevent fluids from leaking into lung alveoli. It is the premier emergency pharmaceutical intervention for High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE).

Usage: Administered alongside supplemental oxygen and immediate descent when a trekker exhibits persistent gurgling breath, blue lips/fingernails (cyanosis), severe resting breathlessness, and pink frothy sputum.

🍬

Throat Lozenges & "Khumbu Cough" Soothers

Strepsils, Fisherman's Friend, Honey-Ginger Lozenges, Buff/Buff Neck Gaiter
Over The Counter High Comfort

Why You Need It: The famous "Khumbu Cough" is caused by hyperventilating dry, sub-freezing Himalayan air for 6 hours a day. It is an airway irritation, not a bacterial chest infection. Menthol or antiseptic lozenges soothe the trachea and lubricate raw vocal cords.

🩹 2. Pain Relief & Anti-Inflammatories

Essential

Trekking 6 to 8 hours daily over steep boulder staircases places immense strain on knees, hips, and lower backs. Mild tension headaches are also routine when ascending through varying atmospheric pressures.

💊

Ibuprofen (Advil / Brufen 400mg)

Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug (NSAID)
Must Pack OTC Everywhere
Standard Dose 400mg every 6–8 hours
Max Daily Dose 1,200mg (OTC limit)
Best For Joint strain & altitude headache
Important Note Always take with food

Why It's the Trail MVP: Unlike simple painkillers, Ibuprofen reduces active tissue inflammation in patellar tendons, IT bands, and knee joints after steep 1,000m descents. Studies have also demonstrated that Ibuprofen can effectively relieve mild altitude-induced headaches without interacting negatively with Diamox.

⚠️ Stomach Safety: Never take Ibuprofen on an empty stomach. Always consume after Dal Bhat, porridge, or trail snacks to prevent gastric irritation.
💊

Paracetamol / Acetaminophen (Panadol / Crocin 500mg)

Analgesic & Antipyretic • Stomach-Friendly Fever & Pain Relief
OTC Stomach Gentle

Role: Ideal for trekkers with peptic ulcers, acid reflux, or sensitive stomachs who cannot tolerate NSAIDs. Excellent for relieving mild evening body aches and lowering fevers during viral colds.

Dosage: 500mg to 1,000mg every 4 to 6 hours as needed. Do not exceed 4,000mg in 24 hours to protect liver function.

🧴

Topical Muscle Rub / Diclofenac Gel (Voltarol / Tiger Balm)

Topical Anti-Inflammatory Cream & Counterirritant
OTC Fast Relief

Targeted relief rubbed directly onto stiff calves, quadriceps, and lower back muscles before sleeping in cold teahouses.

🫃 3. Gastrointestinal Health & Stomach Remedies

High Priority

Changes in altitude, spices, dietary fiber, and rustic teahouse kitchen hygiene routinely challenge gut microbiomes. Carry a dedicated gastro pouch in your daypack.

💧

Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS / Electral Sachets)

WHO Standard Electrolyte Formulation • Sodium, Potassium, Glucose
Lifesaver NPR 25/sachet
Daily Mix 1 sachet per 1L drinking water
Timing Every afternoon on arrival
Key Benefit Prevents altitude hypovolemia
Diarrhea Protocol Drink 250ml after every loose stool

Why It Matters at Altitude: High elevation strips moisture from your lungs with every breath, thickening blood and triggering dehydration-induced headaches that mimic altitude sickness. Plain water alone flushes vital sodium; ORS restores cellular fluid balance rapidly.

🛑

Loperamide (Imodium 2mg)

Anti-Motility Symptomatic Agent
Trail Emergency OTC

When to Use: Only for temporary symptomatic containment when you must walk 5 hours between mountain villages with no toilet facilities. Initial dose is 4mg (2 capsules), followed by 2mg after each unformed stool (maximum 16mg/day).

⚠️ Clinical Caveat: Never take Loperamide if your diarrhea is accompanied by a high fever, severe shivering, or visible blood/mucus in stool. In such cases, trapping invasive bacteria inside the intestinal tract can cause serious toxic megacolon.
💊

Prescription Antibiotic (Azithromycin 500mg or Ciprofloxacin 500mg)

Broad-Spectrum Enteric Antibiotic for Bacterial Gastroenteritis
Prescription Required High Priority
Azithromycin Dose 500mg daily for 3 days
Ciprofloxacin Dose 500mg twice daily for 3 days
Indication Fever + watery cramps >24h
CIWEC Preference Azithromycin preferred for Nepal

Medical Note: The renowned CIWEC Hospital of Travel Medicine in Kathmandu notes significant bacterial resistance in Nepal to older fluoroquinolones (Cipro). Azithromycin is currently the preferred single-course antibiotic for severe bacterial dysentery in the Himalayas. Obtain a 3-day treatment pack from your travel clinic.

🦠

Metronidazole (Flagyl 400mg)

Antiprotozoal Agent for Giardia Lamblia
Prescription

When Indicated: Classic Giardiasis symptoms develop 1–2 weeks into a trek: watery explosive diarrhea accompanied by sulfuric "rotten egg" burps, severe bloating, and fatigue. Standard dose: 400mg to 500mg three times daily for 5 to 7 days.

🍋

Antacids & Anti-Nausea (Omeprazole 20mg / Ondansetron / Avomine)

Proton Pump Inhibitors & Antiemetics
OTC Available

Omeprazole (20mg daily) prevents acid reflux caused by spicy teahouse chilies and hot garlic soups. Ondansetron (4mg sublingual dissolving wafers) is the gold standard antiemetic for rapid vomiting cessation.

👣 4. Blister Prevention & Wound Care

Trail Critical

Foot friction causes more trek dropouts than almost any other condition. The secret to happy trekking in Nepal is pre-emptive taping before a blister forms.

🩹

Compeed Hydrocolloid Blister Plasters

Active Gel Moist Wound Healing Barrier
Must Have Pack Multiple Sizes

Why They Work: Compeed cushions the dermis with hydrocolloid gel, absorbing blister fluid while creating an artificial second skin that seals out Himalayan trail dust. Once applied, leave them in place until they detach naturally after 3 to 5 days.

🥋

Zinc Oxide Strapping Tape / Leukotape P

Rigid High-Adhesion Friction Shield
Sherpa Favorite

The Hotspot Rule: If you feel friction or heat on your heels 15 minutes into a morning walk, stop immediately and apply 2 strips of Leukotape or zinc oxide tape directly over the skin. The tape absorbs all boot friction so your skin doesn't tear.

🩸

Antiseptic Betadine Ointment & Sterile Gauze Swabs

Povidone Iodine 10% Ointment + Non-Adherent Sterile Pads
Infection Shield

At subzero elevations, minor abrasions heal slowly and pick up bacterial infections quickly from yak dung dust on trails. Wash cuts thoroughly with purified water, apply Betadine ointment, and cover with sterile gauze.

🩼

Elastic Crepe Compression Bandage (7.5cm & 10cm)

Heavy-Duty Joint Stabilization Wrap
Joint Protection

Essential for providing compression and structural support following an accidental rolled ankle on loose scree or stabilizing patellar tendon pain on steep descents.

✂️

Trauma Shears, Precision Tweezers & Safety Pins

Essential First Aid Hardware
Hardware

Blunt-tip shears cleanly slice tough zinc oxide tape, clothing, or moleskin. Tweezers extract wood splinters and trail grit from scrapes.

💧 5. Water Sanitation & Purification

Non-Negotiable

Never consume untreated tap, spring, or river water in Nepal. All tap water in mountain teahouses is piped from surface glacier melt or local streams that graze livestock upstream.

🧪

Chlorine Dioxide Purification Tablets (Aquatabs / Micropur Forte)

Gold Standard Chemical Disinfection • Neutralizes Viruses, Bacteria & Hard Cysts
WHO / CDC Gold Standard Lightweight

Why Chlorine Dioxide Wins: Unlike old-fashioned iodine or standard chlorine, Chlorine Dioxide kills tough protozoan cysts (Cryptosporidium parvum and Giardia) without leaving an unpleasant chemical odor or metallic aftertaste.

Contact Timing: Add 1 tablet per 1 liter of tap water. Wait 30 minutes for general bacteria and viruses; allow 2 to 4 hours if treating freezing, turbid stream water known to harbor Cryptosporidium.

🥤

Hollow-Fiber Squeeze Filter (Sawyer Squeeze / Katadyn BeFree) or UV SteriPen

Instant Physical Filtration / Ultraviolet Sterilization
Zero Waiting Time

Instant Drinking Water: Squeeze filters mechanically filter out 99.9999% of bacteria and protozoa in seconds, eliminating the 30-minute waiting period of chemical tablets.

❄️ Critical Subzero Warning: If wet water inside a hollow-fiber filter freezes overnight, the ice expands and microscopic pores shatter, permanently ruining the filter with zero visual indication! Above 3,500m, always sleep with your filter inside your sleeping bag to prevent internal freezing.

🚨 High-Altitude Illness Diagnostic Triage (AMS vs HAPE vs HACE)

Altitude illness exists on a spectrum from mild discomfort to medical emergencies. Learn to distinguish between early warning signs and critical descent triggers:

Level 1: Warning ⛰️

Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS)

Like an altitude hangover. Common above 3,000m.

  • • Dull frontal or throbbing headache
  • • Mild loss of appetite / nausea
  • • Unusual fatigue & sleep disturbance
  • • Dizziness when standing abruptly
Mandatory Protocol: Do NOT ascend. Rest at same altitude. Hydrate with 1L ORS, take 400mg Ibuprofen and continue Diamox 125mg twice daily. If symptoms resolve within 24 hours, normal ascent may resume.
Level 2: Critical 🫁

High Altitude Pulmonary Edema (HAPE)

Fluid leaking into the lungs. #1 altitude killer.

  • • Severe breathlessness at complete rest
  • • Persistent dry cough progressing to wet cough
  • • Gurgling / rattling lung sounds (crackles)
  • • Blue or gray lips & fingertips (cyanosis)
  • • Extreme physical exhaustion & weakness
Emergency Action: IMMEDIATE DESCENT MANDATORY (at least 500m–1,000m drop). Administer high-flow bottled oxygen and Nifedipine. Call Nepal Unique Treks 24/7 Kathmandu operations for helicopter extraction.
Level 3: Fatal Emergency 🧠

High Altitude Cerebral Edema (HACE)

Severe fluid swelling of the brain.

  • • Loss of balance & ataxia ("drunk walker")
  • • Confusion, apathy, slurred speech
  • • Severe, incapacitating headache
  • • Hallucinations or irrational behavior
  • • Coma if untreated within 12–24 hours
Life-Saving Action: DESCEND IMMEDIATELY EVEN AT NIGHT. Administer Dexamethasone 8mg stat + supplemental oxygen. Put patient in Gamow hyperbaric chamber if available. Helicopter rescue non-negotiable.
1
Golden Rule #1

Any illness, headache, or malaise at high altitude is considered Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) until proven otherwise.

2
Golden Rule #2

Never ascend to a higher sleeping elevation with unresolved symptoms of Acute Mountain Sickness.

3
Golden Rule #3

If symptoms worsen at the current altitude or signs of HAPE/HACE develop, descend immediately without hesitation.

4
Golden Rule #4

Never leave a sick trekker alone. Always send a licensed Sherpa guide or assistant to accompany any descending trekker.

🛒 Buying Medications in Kathmandu & Thamel

Forgot to pack something? Kathmandu's trekking hub of Thamel has dozens of reliable, well-stocked pharmacies where basic high-altitude medications can be purchased at very economical rates:

📍 Where to Shop

Head to pharmacies along JP Road, Thamel Marg (opposite Kathmandu Guest House), or Chhetrapati Chowk. Most pharmacists speak fluent English and cater specifically to Everest and Annapurna mountaineers.

💵 Typical Local Prices

Acetazolamide (Diamox 250mg): ~NPR 250–350 ($2–$3) for a strip of 10. Electral ORS: ~NPR 25 ($0.20) per sachet. Ibuprofen 400mg: ~NPR 60 ($0.50) per strip. Compeed blister packs: ~NPR 900–1,200 ($7–$9).

🔍 Check Expiry & Seals

Always inspect the stamped expiration date on the foil blister pack. Ensure individual pill blisters are intact and unbroken. Avoid loose unsealed tablets sold in bulk plastic bags.

🏥 Travel Clinics

For specialized travel vaccines, rabies boosters, or physician consultations, visit CIWEC Hospital Travel Medicine Center (Lazimpat) or Era International Hospital (Sorhakhutte, Thamel).
