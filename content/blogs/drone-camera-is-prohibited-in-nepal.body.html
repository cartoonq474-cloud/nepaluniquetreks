Do You Need a Drone Permit in Nepal?

Yes! The Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal (CAAN) regulates drone usage in Nepal. Whether you're a tourist, a professional filmmaker, or a local enthusiast, you must obtain a drone permit before flying. Unauthorized drone flights can lead to confiscation and fines.

Types of Drone Permits in Nepal

Drone permits vary depending on the purpose of use. Here are the main categories:

  • Recreational Drone Use: If you want to fly a drone for personal use, you still need a permit, but the process is relatively simpler.

  • Commercial Drone Use: Photographers, videographers, and researchers using drones for commercial purposes must go through a more detailed approval process.

  • Journalistic and Research Use: News agencies and research organizations require additional permits, often needing approval from multiple authorities.

Drone Regulations in Nepal

Before flying a drone, it is essential to be aware of the regulations imposed by CAAN:

  • Drones weighing more than 2 kg require special permission.

  • You cannot fly above 120 meters (400 feet) above the ground level.

  • Drones must not be flown near airports, military areas, or government buildings.

  • Flying over heritage sites like Pashupatinath, Boudhanath, and Swayambhunath requires additional permits from the Department of Archaeology.

  • Pilots must maintain a visual line of sight with their drone at all times.

  • Flying over Everest Base Camp Trek and other restricted trekking areas requires special permission from local authorities and conservation areas.

How to Apply for a Drone Permit in Nepal

The drone permit application involves several steps. Here’s how you can obtain one:

Step 1: Submit an Application to CAAN

Prepare an application with details of your drone, the purpose of the flight, and planned locations. Submit this to the Civil Aviation Authority of Nepal (CAAN).

Step 2: Get Approval from the Ministry of Home Affairs

For foreigners, additional approval from the Ministry of Home Affairs is required, especially if using a drone for commercial purposes.

Step 3: Obtain Local Permissions

If you plan to fly your drone in national parks, trekking regions, or heritage sites, you need approval from relevant authorities such as Sagarmatha National Park, Annapurna Conservation Area, or the Department of Archaeology.

Step 4: Pay the Required Fees

Permit fees vary based on drone weight, purpose, and location. The charges can range from $100 to $500 or more, depending on the area and duration of use.

Step 5: Receive Your Permit

Once all approvals are secured, CAAN will issue your official drone permit. Make sure to carry a copy while flying your drone in Nepal.

Consequences of Flying a Drone Without a Permit

Unauthorized drone flying in Nepal can result in heavy fines, drone confiscation, or legal consequences. The authorities take drone regulations seriously to protect air safety, national security, and cultural heritage sites.

Best Locations for Drone Photography in Nepal

  • Annapurna Region Trekking (including Ghorepani, Poon Hill, and Mardi Himal)

  • Pokhara and Phewa Lake

  • Rara Lake

  • Kathmandu Valley (with restrictions around heritage sites)
